# Week 12: Sales & Operations Planning (S&OP)

## 🎯 Executive Summary

Sales & Operations Planning (S&OP) is an executive consensus process that reconciles unconstrained commercial market demand with operational capacity constraints, financial budgets, and supply chain realities. It creates a single, unified operating plan for the entire enterprise.

---

## 1. Aggregate Planning Strategies

Balancing supply and demand across a medium-term horizon (3–18 months) at the product-family level:

### 1. Chase Strategy (Match Demand)

- Match production rates strictly to order rates by hiring and laying off workers as demand fluctuates.
- **Benefits**: Zero excess inventory holding costs.
- **Drawbacks**: High hiring/firing severance costs, workforce demoralization, quality instability.

### 2. Level Strategy (Constant Output)

- Maintain a stable workforce and constant production rate. Demand variations are absorbed by building inventory during troughs and depleting it during demand surges (or backordering).
- **Benefits**: Stable workforce, high employee morale, consistent equipment utilization.
- **Drawbacks**: Massive inventory carrying costs and potential obsolescence risks.

### 3. Hybrid / Mixed Strategy

- A realistic combination: baseline level production augmented with overtime shifts, subcontracting, and temporary seasonal labor.

---

## 2. The Monthly 5-Step S&OP Governance Cycle

```
  Step 1: Data Gathering (Sales history, inventory actuals, backlog)
          │
          ▼
  Step 2: Demand Planning (Unconstrained consensus sales forecast)
          │
          ▼
  Step 3: Supply Planning (Capacity feasibility, procurement check)
          │
          ▼
  Step 4: Pre-S&OP Meeting (Reconciling financial gaps & supply bottlenecks)
          │
          ▼
  Step 5: Executive S&OP Meeting (C-Suite sign-off on allocation & P&L commit)
```
