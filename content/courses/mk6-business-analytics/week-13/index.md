# Week 13: Business Intelligence (BI) & Executive Visualization

## 🎯 Executive Summary

Executive decision-makers operate under severe cognitive load. Visual analytics and Business Intelligence (BI) dashboards transform complex multidimensional data into intuitive visual hierarchies that highlight anomalies, guide attention, and accelerate strategic decision velocity.

---

## 1. The Science of Visual Perception & Information Design

### Pre-Attentive Visual Attributes (Bertin & Ware)

The human visual cortex processes certain visual attributes instantaneously (in under 250 milliseconds) before conscious cognitive processing begins:

- **Position & Length**: Highest quantitative precision (bar charts, scatter plots).
- **Size & Area**: Moderate precision (avoid 3D charts, pie charts with > 4 slices).
- **Color Hue & Intensity**: Highly effective for categorical grouping and anomaly alerting, but easily overused.

### Tufte's Principles of Analytical Design

- **Data-Ink Ratio**: Maximize the share of ink/pixels dedicated to displaying core data; ruthlessly eliminate "chartjunk" (decorative borders, 3D effects, artificial gradients).
- **Lie Factor**: The size of an effect shown in the graphic divided by the size of the effect in the underlying data. Must equal approximately 1.0 (never truncate bar chart baselines to exaggerate minor differences).

---

## 2. Designing Executive Dashboards

- The **5-Second Rule**: An executive should grasp the primary performance trajectory within 5 seconds of scanning the dashboard.
- The **F-Pattern & Visual Hierarchy**: Place critical macro KPIs in the top-left quadrant; place trend lines in the center; place granular breakdowns at the bottom.
- Connecting BI dashboards to operational action: Every red warning KPI must have an explicit drill-down to root causes and assigned operational owners.
