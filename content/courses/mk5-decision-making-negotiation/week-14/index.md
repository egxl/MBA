# Week 14: Multiparty Negotiation & Coalition Building

## 🎯 Executive Summary

Executive leadership rarely negotiates one-on-one. Real-world corporate transactions, ministry alignments, and BUMN consortiums involve **multiparty negotiations** where shifting coalitions, voting rules, and procedural management determine the ultimate outcome.

---

## 1. Multiparty Dynamics & Coalition Architecture

### Key Distinctions from Two-Party Bargaining

- **Coalition Formation**: Subsets of parties can unite to pool voting power and dictate terms to non-coalition members.
- **Informational Complexity**: Asymmetric knowledge distributed among multiple actors creates echo chambers and strategic leaks.
- **Procedural Chaos**: Who speaks first? How is the agenda established? Are decisions made by unanimous consent or majority rule?

### Coalition Mechanics & Game Theory

- **Winning Coalition**: Minimum subset of actors required to pass a binding resolution.
- **Blocking Coalition**: Minimum subset capable of vetoing a resolution.
- **Instability of Coalitions**: Coalitions are inherently unstable; excluded parties will offer attractive side-payments to induce a coalition member to defect.
- **Core Rule**: To maintain a coalition, ensure that surplus is distributed equitably according to each member's Shapley value (marginal contribution).

## 2. MK 5 Capstone Synthesis: The Master Problem Solver & Negotiator

Review of the 14-week analytical progression:

1. Grounding in BATNA, ZOPA, and reservation values.
2. De-biasing System 1 heuristics through rational choice architecture.
3. Rigorous Kepner-Tregoe diagnosis: Situation Appraisal, Problem Analysis (IS/IS NOT), Decision Analysis (MUSTs/WANTs), and PPA.
4. Multi-criteria quantification using SMART utility scoring and Saaty's Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP).
5. Systems Thinking (causal loops) and 2x2 Scenario Planning for macro uncertainty.
6. Value Claiming (anchoring, concessions) vs. Value Creation (principled bargaining, MESOs, logrolling, contingency contracts).
7. Managing complex multi-stakeholder coalitions.
