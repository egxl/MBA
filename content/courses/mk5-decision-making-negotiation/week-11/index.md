# Week 11: Claiming Value ("Slicing the Pie") & Anchoring Tactics

## 🎯 Executive Summary

In pure single-issue distributive bargaining (e.g., buying a commodity or settling an insurance claim), value creation is impossible; every dollar gained by one party is a dollar surrendered by the other. Week 11 analyzes the mechanics of **Anchoring**, **First Offers**, and **Concession Management**.

---

## 1. The Science of First Offers & Anchoring

### Should You Make the First Offer?

- **Yes, when you possess superior information**: Psychological research demonstrates that the final settlement correlates with the first offer at $r \approx 0.70$ to $0.85$. First offers establish a powerful psychological anchor that pulls counter-offers toward it.
- **No, when you face severe information asymmetry**: Making the first offer risks making a "curse offer" (offering terms far more generous than the counterparty expected).

### How to Anchor Effectively

1. **Ambitious yet Justifiable**: Anchor at the edge of the plausible range, backed by a credible standard of legitimacy (market comps, precedent transactions). Ridiculous anchors destroy credibility.
2. **Precise Numbers**: Proposing $\$1,485,000$ anchors far more effectively than $\$1,500,000$. Precise numbers signal detailed cost modeling and leave less psychological room for aggressive counter-anchors.

## 2. Disciplined Concession Patterns

```
  Poor Pattern (Escalating Concessions):
  Initial: $100K ──► Concession 1: -$5K ──► Concession 2: -$15K ──► Concession 3: -$30K
  (Signals to counterpart: "Keep pushing, they are breaking!")

  Optimal Pattern (Decreasing Concessions):
  Initial: $100K ──► Concession 1: -$20K ──► Concession 2: -$8K ──► Concession 3: -$2K
  (Signals to counterpart: "We are rapidly approaching our hard reservation floor.")
```

- **The Reciprocity Rule of Concessions**: Never concede unilaterally. Every concession must be explicitly contingent: _"I can consider shifting delivery terms, but only if you agree to 15-day payment terms."_
