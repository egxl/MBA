# Week 12: Transitioning from Distributive to Integrative Negotiation

## 🎯 Executive Summary

Single-issue negotiations inevitably trap parties in positional bargaining. **Integrative Negotiation** (Principled Negotiation / Harvard Negotiation Project) transforms zero-sum disputes into cooperative problem-solving by shifting from rigid _positions_ to underlying _interests_.

---

## 1. Positional Bargaining vs. Principled Negotiation (Fisher & Ury)

```
  Positional Bargaining                Principled (Integrative) Negotiation
  ┌─────────────────────────┐          ┌─────────────────────────┐
  │ "I demand $500 per ton" │   vs.    │ "What are your storage, │
  │ Rigid, ego-entrenched,  │          │ cash flow, and purity   │
  │ compromise-focused      │          │ constraints?"           │
  └─────────────────────────┘          └─────────────────────────┘
```

### The 4 Pillars of Principled Negotiation

1. **Separate the People from the Problem**: Be soft on the people, but extraordinarily firm on the problem. Build psychological safety and trust while holding ground on commercial objectives.
2. **Focus on Interests, Not Positions**: Positions are what you say you want; interests are _why_ you want them (desires, fears, economic realities). Behind opposed positions lie shared and compatible interests.
3. **Invent Options for Mutual Gain**: Brainstorm multi-issue packages _before_ attempting to reach agreements. Expand the pie before slicing it.
4. **Insist on Objective Criteria**: Anchor terms to independent benchmarks (fair market value, replacement costs, legal precedents, industry standards) rather than battles of will.

---

## 2. Uncovering Latent Interests

- Use open-ended diagnostic questioning: _"Help me understand why that delivery timeline is critical for your executive committee?"_
- Active listening and perspective-taking: Paraphrasing the counterparty's core concerns until they validate: _"Yes, you understand our dilemma."_
