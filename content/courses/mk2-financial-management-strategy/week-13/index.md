# Week 13: Financial Risk Management & Risk Governance

## 🎯 Executive Summary

Unmanaged financial market volatility (currency fluctuations, interest rate spikes, commodity price shocks) can destabilize enterprise operations and jeopardize capital expenditure plans. Corporate risk management uses derivative instruments and enterprise risk frameworks to protect corporate cash flows.

---

## 1. Core Frameworks & Risk Architecture

### Total Risk Decomposition

$$\text{Total Risk} = \text{Systematic Risk (Market / Undiversifiable)} + \text{Unsystematic Risk (Firm-Specific / Diversifiable)}$$

- **Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)**:
  $$r_e = r_f + \beta_i (r_m - r_f)$$
  Beta ($\beta$) measures the sensitivity of an asset's returns relative to market index fluctuations.

### Primary Financial Risk Categories

1. **Foreign Exchange (FX) Risk**:
   - _Transaction Exposure_: Outstanding receivables or payables denominated in foreign currency.
   - _Translation Exposure_: Consolidating foreign subsidiary balance sheets into home reporting currency.
   - _Operating (Economic) Exposure_: Long-term shifts in real exchange rates affecting competitive positioning.
2. **Interest Rate Risk**: Asset-liability maturity mismatches and floating-rate debt exposure.
3. **Commodity Price Risk**: Input price volatility (fuel, agricultural commodities, industrial metals).

### Corporate Hedging Instruments

- **Forward Contracts**: Custom OTC binding agreements to buy/sell at a set price on a future date.
- **Futures Contracts**: Standardized, exchange-traded contracts marked to market daily.
- **Swaps**: Exchanging cash flows (e.g., plain-vanilla interest rate swaps: floating-for-fixed).
- **Options**: Purchasing asymmetric protection (Caps, Floors, Collars) with limited downside and upside participation.
