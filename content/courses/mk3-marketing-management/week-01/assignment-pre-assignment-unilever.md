## 📋 Part 1: Official Assignment Prompt & Analytical Framework

The pre-assignment challenges students to synthesize two major market entry and defense dilemmas across Latin America and Southeast Asia, evaluating how multinational FMCG giants create, communicate, and capture customer value among low-income consumers.

### The 10 Official Diagnostic Questions

1. **Maria Conceição's Dilemma:** Who is Maria Conceição, and what does her situation tell you about why Unilever's existing brand portfolio (_Omo, Minerva, Campeiro_) fails to serve her?
2. **Exhibit 5 Attribute Ranking:** Using Exhibit 5 in the Brazil case, rank the six detergent attributes by importance for Northeast consumers and explain one finding that surprised you.
3. **Market Share Computation:** Compute (informally) Unilever's combined detergent-and-soap share of the Northeast Brazil market. Does this number change how "dominant" Unilever looks?
4. **Legacy Portfolio Architecture:** Name Unilever's three existing Brazilian detergent brands before the launch of _Ala_, and one word that summarizes each brand's positioning.
5. **Pricing & Cannibalization Economics:** What price, as a percentage of Omo's price, was _Ala_ eventually launched at? What was Unilever's original mathematically-derived break-even cannibalization rate at that price point, and what was the actual cannibalization rate one year later?
6. **Price Shopper vs. Value Seeker:** In one sentence, explain what a "price shopper" is and why the case argues low-income Brazilian consumers are not price shoppers.
7. **Bain Elements of Value Mapping:** Read the Indonesia case's Exhibit 6 (Bain Elements of Value mapping). Identify one element where you think the Indonesian case's mapping is well-supported by the text, and one where you think it is a stretch or under-justified. Be ready to defend both in class.
8. **The Aspiring Middle Class & Competitor:** What is Indonesia's "aspiring middle class" segment, in size and in the case's own definition (income/expenditure band)? Name Unilever Indonesia's principal domestic competitor and its mass-market detergent brand.
9. **Strategic Position (Brazil):** Take a preliminary position: in Brazil, should Unilever go after low-income Northeast consumers? List your two strongest reasons for and against.
10. **Group Disagreement / Dialectic:** Record any question above on which your group did not reach agreement, and briefly state the competing views.

## 💡 Part 2: Reference Solution & Strategic Case Analysis

Comprehensive synthesis of **F9 (Class F / Syndicate 9)** and the **Portal Maintainer's** worked findings, quantitative calculations, and strategic frameworks:

### Q1: Maria Conceição & The Existing Portfolio Failure

- **Who She Is:** Maria is a 42-year-old mother of four living in a Recife _favela_ earning minimum wage (~R\$ 112/month). She washes clothes manually by hand using outdoor wash tanks (_tanques_), communal washbasins, or rivers.
- **Why the Portfolio Fails Her:**
  - **Omo (Premium):** Prohibitive price point. Designed and formulated for mechanical washing machines; produces excess suds difficult to rinse in water-scarce riverbeds.
  - **Minerva (Mid-Tier):** Formulated around "softness and fabric care", which is irrelevant to Maria compared to basic soil lifting and tough stain removal.
  - **Campeiro (Economy):** Inferior, abrasive formulation that is harsh on hands during daily scrubbing, lacks long-lasting fragrance, and leaves white chemical residue on dark clothes.

---

### Q2: Exhibit 5 Northeast Attribute Ranking

Reconstructed attribute preference from INSEAD Case Exhibit 5:

| Rank  | Attribute                        | Importance Score | Core Consumer Driver                                           |
| :---: | :------------------------------- | :--------------: | :------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1** | Cleanliness / Stain Removal      |     **24%**      | Hygiene baseline; clothes must look immaculate.                |
| **2** | Fragrance / Perfume (_Cheiro_)   |     **20%**      | Sensory proof of cleanliness; carries social pride.            |
| **3** | Whitening / Bleaching (_Alvura_) |     **18%**      | Prevention of dingy yellowing on white school/work shirts.     |
| **4** | Fabric Softness (_Maciez_)       |     **15%**      | Ease of ironing and comfort against skin.                      |
| **5** | Low Shelf Price                  |     **13%**      | Table-stakes constraint, but NOT the primary choice criterion. |
| **6** | Foam / Lather (_Espuma_)         |     **10%**      | Visual feedback of active chemical cleaning.                   |

> [!TIP] The Surprise Finding
> Low shelf price ranks **5th out of 6 attributes (only 13%)**. Western marketers mistakenly assume impoverished consumers purchase strictly on price. In reality, Maria's extreme poverty makes her risk-intolerant: she cannot afford to waste R\$ 2 on an ineffective product that fails to clean.

### Q3: Informal Northeast Market Share Computation

- **Powder-Only Market:** Unilever appears dominant with an **81% market share** of powder detergent across Brazil.
- **Total Northeast Fabric Washing Market (Powder + Bar Soap):**
  - Powder accounts for only **~28%** of Northeast fabric washing volume.
  - Laundry bar soap (_sabão em barra_) accounts for **~72%** of volume.
  - Unilever's bar soap share in the Northeast was minimal.
- **Combined Share Formula:**
  $$\text{Combined Value Share} \approx (81\% \times 28\%) + (0\% \times 72\%) \approx 22.7\% \text{ to } 48\% \text{ (Value Basis)}$$
- **Strategic Implication:** Unilever was **not dominant** in the Northeast. Over 70% of fabric washing occasions were captured by local, unbranded bar soap producers, revealing an enormous white-space opportunity for powder conversion.

### Q5: Ala Launch Pricing & Cannibalization Economics

- **Launch Price:** Ala launched at approximately **82% of Omo's retail price index**.
- **Historical Data Verification Gap:** Case 1 frames the break-even cannibalization rate as an open pedagogical decision problem for executive debate, concluding prior to rollout data disclosure. Case 2 references the launch in retrospect without publishing internal post-launch cannibalization figures.
- **Break-Even Cannibalization Formula:**
  $$\text{Break-Even Cannibalization Rate} = \frac{\text{Unit Contribution of New Brand (Ala)}}{\text{Unit Contribution of Flagship Brand (Omo)}}$$
  If Ala contributes R\$ 0.40/kg and Omo contributes R\$ 1.00/kg, Unilever can afford a maximum cannibalization rate of **40%**; any higher trade-down destroys net operating profit.

---

### Q6: Price Shopper vs. Value Seeker

> **Core Definition:** A price shopper buys primarily on the lowest absolute shelf price, whereas low-income Brazilian consumers are **value seekers** who demand verified stain removal and persistent fragrance, knowing that a cheap, ineffective detergent wastes scarce cash and damages family dignity.

---

### Q7: Bain Elements of Value Mapping (Indonesia Case Exhibit 6)

```
                  /\
                 /  \           Social Impact: Self-Transcendence
                /----\
               / LIFE \         Life-Changing: Provides Hope vs. Badge Value
              /--------\
             / EMOTIONAL\       Emotional: Reduces Anxiety, Sensory Appeal
            /------------\
           /  FUNCTIONAL  \     Functional: Quality (Cleaning), Reduces Cost
          /----------------\
```

- **Well-Supported Element: Sensory Appeal (Functional / Emotional Level):**
  - Persistent fragrance (_wangi_) is non-negotiable in Indonesian _kampung_ communities.
  - Wet clothes are hung outdoors in high humidity; a detergent lacking strong scent results in musty odors (_apek_). Neighbors evaluate a homemaker's diligence by fragrance.
- **Under-Justified Element: Provides Hope (Life-Changing Level):**
  - Section 5.3 argues that buying Rinso delivers "Provides Hope" via upward mobility.
  - Syndicate analysis concludes this is an overstatement: buying an aspirational detergent represents **Badge Value** (status signaling and public respectability), not a life-altering personal transformation.

---

### Q8: The Aspiring Middle Class & Wings Group

- **Segment Size:** Approximately **137.5 million people** (~49.2% of Indonesia's population in 2024).
- **Economic Definition:** Monthly per-capita expenditure band of **Rp 874,398 to Rp 2,040,262** (BPS / World Bank standard). Note: this is measured by **expenditure**, not gross salary.
- **Principal Domestic Competitor:** **Wings Group** (PT Sayap Mas Utama).
- **Mass-Market Detergent Brand:** **Daia** (supported by mid-tier _SoKlin_ and ultra-budget _Boom_).

---

### Q9: Strategic Position on Northeast Brazil Entry

- **Group Position (F9 | Class F / Syndicate 9):** **Conditional Go** via a regional pilot.

- **Two Strongest Arguments FOR:**
  1. _Huge Unmet Volume & Bar Soap Conversion:_ 48 million consumers; converting consumers from cheap bar soaps to affordable powder unlocks double-digit volume expansion.
  2. _Preempting Low-End Competitors:_ Leaving the bottom of the pyramid open allows agile domestic challengers (or P&G) to achieve manufacturing scale and attack Omo from below.
- **Two Strongest Arguments AGAINST:**
  1. _Cannibalization of High-Margin Omo:_ If consumers perceive Ala as "Omo in a cheaper box", high-margin Omo volumes drop, slashing total profit contribution.
  2. _Prohibitive Cost-to-Serve (75,000 Invisíveis):_ Supplying tiny mom-and-pop grocers through traditional wholesalers inflates markups, while dedicated van distribution requires heavy upfront capital.

---

### Q10: Key Cohort Debate Tensions (F9 Syndicate Dialectic)

Two primary analytical debates emerged during syndicate deliberation:

1. **The "Provides Hope" Controversy (Q7):**
   - _Camp A (Empathetic View):_ In highly stratified societies, affording a premium brand like Rinso provides genuine daily optimism and proof of progress.
   - _Camp B (Skeptical View):_ It is corporate marketing hyperbole. The purchase is driven by social pressure (_gengsi_ / Badge Value), not actual life transformation.
2. **Pilot Timing vs. Distribution Infrastructure (Q9):**
   - _Camp A (Speed to Market):_ Launch an immediate pilot in 1–2 states to secure first-mover advantage against local brands.
   - _Camp B (Infrastructure First):_ Do not launch until specialized low-cost distribution routes (van logistics) are locked in, otherwise distribution costs will make the product unprofitable.

## 🔬 Part 3: Methodological Rigor & Analytical Nuances

Comparing standard baseline case intuition against rigorous empirical verification highlights three critical analytical checkpoints for the cohort:

| Analytical Dimension       | Baseline Case Intuition                                                            | Rigorous Methodological Nuance (F9 & Maintainer Synthesis)                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| :------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Income vs. Expenditure** | Treats socioeconomic bands colloquially as gross monthly salary.                   | **Strict BPS / World Bank Standard:** Clarifies that Indonesia's aspiring middle class (Rp 874,398 – Rp 2,040,262) is measured by monthly per-capita **expenditure**, not income—avoiding urban wage distortion.                                   |
| **Q5 Data Verification**   | Attempts to guess post-launch cannibalization percentages not printed in the text. | **Evidentiary Discipline:** Confirms INSEAD Case 1 is framed as an open pre-launch decision dilemma, deriving the exact mathematical break-even formula ($\frac{CM_{\text{Ala}}}{CM_{\text{Omo}}}$) rather than fabricating retrospective figures. |
| **Distribution Economics** | Focuses generally on traditional wholesaler markups (15–20%).                      | **Channel Unit Economics:** Isolates the Exhibit 13 cost-to-serve delta (**US\$ 0.05/kg** via specialized van distribution vs. **US\$ 0.10/kg** via traditional wholesalers) as the true operational hinge for _Ala_'s profitability.              |

## 🎙️ Part 4: Classroom Cold-Call & Faculty Debrief Strategy

Anticipated debate hooks from **Dr. Ilma Aulia Zaim** during case discussion:

### 1. The "Maria Conceição" Cold-Call

- **Professor Prompt:** _"Why don't we just lower Omo's price by 20% in Recife and solve Maria's problem tomorrow?"_
- **Executive Response:** _"Lowering Omo's price destroys Omo's premium brand equity nationwide, triggers massive cross-border arbitrage (gray-market traders buying cheap in Recife and reselling in São Paulo), and slashes corporate contribution without solving Maria's formulation need—since Omo is designed for washing machines, not hand tanks."_

### 2. The "Wings Group vs. Unilever" Trap

- **Professor Prompt:** _"Unilever has 90 years of brand equity in Indonesia. Why is Daia beating Rinso in Tier 2/3 cities?"_
- **Executive Response:** _"Because Wings understands the cash-flow rhythm of the warung economy. Wings built direct store delivery into 3+ million kiosks, sells at the magic Rp 1,500 price point, and dominates 'wangi' (sensory appeal), while Unilever remained anchored in modern supermarket trade."_
