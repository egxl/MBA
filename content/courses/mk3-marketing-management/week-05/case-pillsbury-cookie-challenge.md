## 🚨 The Executive Dilemma

In early 2006, the Canadian marketing leadership at [[entities/general-mills|General Mills, Inc.]] faced a strategic crossroads. In the United States, Pillsbury Refrigerated Cookie Dough (RCD) was a dominant category giant, generating massive recurring household penetration. However, in Canada, the product had languished for over a decade as a marginal niche, with per-capita consumption at less than a third of US levels.

Brand Manager **Peter MacLeod** and Marketing Director **Alison Covey** commissioned four extensive market research studies to diagnose the root causes of the Canadian growth bottleneck. However, the data presented severe contradictions:

- **Survey Data (Descriptive)** showed that Canadian moms loved fresh-baked cookies, placed high emotional value on family baking warmth, and praised Pillsbury’s taste in blind taste tests.
- **Scanner Panel Data (Revealed Checkout Behavior)** showed that when Canadian consumers walked down the refrigerated supermarket dairy aisle, they walked straight past the Pillsbury dough tubes, purchasing shelf-stable boxed cookies or raw scratch-baking ingredients instead.

With management demanding a $30\%$ revenue acceleration or a category phase-out, the brand team had to evaluate the research evidence and decide whether to launch a multi-million dollar repositioning and pack reformulation campaign.

### The Core Consumer Paradox

Canadian consumers baked cookies just as frequently as American consumers ($\sim 18$ occasions per year). However, Canadian baking culture was rooted in **scratch baking from raw flour, butter, and sugar** as an authentic maternal ritual. Pre-formed refrigerated dough tubes were subtly perceived by Canadian home bakers as "cheating" or artificial, whereas American consumers viewed it as smart convenience.

## 📈 Quantitative Exhibits & The Four Evidence Streams

| Evidence Block                                 | Study Design & Scope                                                                                | Core Finding / Data Point                                                                                    | Critical Methodological Limitation                                                                                 |
| :--------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. 2006 Canadian U&A Study**                 | Primary, quantitative, cross-sectional survey ($N = 4,180$ respondents, $N = 3,054$ cookie bakers). | 82% of Canadian bakers agree "warm cookies make home feel cozy"; 64% say taste is top driver.                | Measures stated attitudes, not checkout behavior. Self-report bias conceals embarrassment of using pre-made dough. |
| **2. 2004 US U&A Study**                       | Secondary benchmark survey ($N = 3,361$ US respondents, $N = 2,547$ bakers).                        | US consumers index higher on speed, convenience, and routine weekly snacking.                                | Cross-sectional temporal confounding (2004 US vs 2006 Canada); different screening quotas.                         |
| **3. In-Home Immersions & Discovery Workshop** | Primary, qualitative ethnography (in-home observations + consumer workshops).                       | Mothers felt guilt using pre-formed chub rolls, but experienced immense relief when baking with children.    | Small unrepresentative sample; high observer Hawthorne effect; cannot size market opportunity.                     |
| **4. Nielsen Scanner Panel Data**              | Objective point-of-sale scanner sales tracking across major Canadian supermarket chains.            | Baseline sales velocity is flat; volume spikes by $+180\%$ only during deep temporary trade price discounts. | Reveals _what_ consumers bought at what price, but cannot explain _why_ non-users never enter the aisle.           |

## 🎯 Executive Recommendation & Implementation Roadmap

**Syndicate 9 Recommendation: Migrate from Chub Tubes to "Place & Bake" Format with Co-Merchandising**

1. **Format Innovation**: Terminate consumer reliance on messy chub tubes (which required slicing with a knife and washing cutting boards, destroying the convenience promise). Transition to pre-cut **"Place & Bake"** dough pucks.
2. **Repositioning Narrative**: Reframe marketing communications from "substitute for scratch baking" to **"fun, stress-free family time."** Frame the product as an enabler of maternal bonding rather than a lazy shortcut.
3. **Retail Secondary Placement**: Negotiate refrigerated secondary clip-strips and mobile end-cap coolers near the bakery and milk sections to break out of the dairy aisle graveyard.

## 🎙️ Classroom Cold-Call & Boardroom Defense

### Cold-Call 1: _"Why shouldn't General Mills simply lower the price in Canada to match the US $1.99 shelf price?"_

> **Defense**: The Nielsen scanner data proves that price discounting creates short-term promotional blips without building baseline trial. Canadian consumers were not avoiding Pillsbury because it cost 50 cents more; they avoided it because the chub format was messy and scratch baking was culturally entrenched. Cutting price merely subsidizes existing loyalists while destroying gross margin needed for category education.
