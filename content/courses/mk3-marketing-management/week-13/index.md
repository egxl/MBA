# Week 13: Strategic Brand Management & Brand Equity

## 🎯 Executive Summary

Brands are intangible economic assets that de-risk future cash flows and command enduring pricing power. **Customer-Based Brand Equity (CBBE)** measures the differential effect that brand knowledge has on consumer response to the marketing of that brand.

---

## 1. Keller's Brand Equity Pyramid (CBBE Model)

Building a strong brand requires four sequential steps:

```
                  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │               4. RESONANCE              │
                  │   Intense, active loyalty & community   │
                  └────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                       │
                  ┌────────────────────▼────────────────────┐
                  │     3. JUDGMENTS   │     FEELINGS       │
                  │  Quality, Credibility│  Warmth, Fun, Pride│
                  └────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                       │
                  ┌────────────────────▼────────────────────┐
                  │    2. PERFORMANCE  │      IMAGERY       │
                  │   Reliability, Price│   History, Values  │
                  └────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                       │
                  ┌────────────────────▼────────────────────┐
                  │               1. SALIENCE               │
                  │     Deep, broad brand awareness (Who)   │
                  └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Brand Identity (Salience)**: Who are you? Ensuring category salience and top-of-mind recall under purchase situations.
2. **Brand Meaning (Performance & Imagery)**: What are you? Defining functional execution and emotional associations.
3. **Brand Responses (Judgments & Feelings)**: What about you? Consumer evaluations of perceived quality, superiority, credibility, and emotional resonance.
4. **Brand Relationships (Resonance)**: What about you and me? Converting brand awareness into fierce loyalty, psychological attachment, and active community engagement.

---

## 2. Brand Architecture Strategies

- **House of Brands (Branded House)**: Single master brand across all categories (e.g., Virgin, Google, GE). Highly efficient, but risks enterprise-wide contagion if one product fails.
- **Branded House (House of Brands)**: Standalone product brands operating independently (e.g., Unilever, Procter & Gamble, Indofood). Protects core enterprise reputation, but demands substantial marketing investment per brand.
- **Endorsed Brands & Sub-Brands**: Hybrid architectures (e.g., Marriott Courtyard, Apple iPhone).
