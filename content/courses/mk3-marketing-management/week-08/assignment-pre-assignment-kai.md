### 2. Fleet Availability Calculations (Case p. 5)

Between 2000 and 2009, severe underinvestment and maintenance deficits crippled KAI's operational rolling-stock availability:

$$\text{Availability Rate} = \frac{\text{Operational Units}}{\text{Total Fleet Units}} \times 100\%$$

- **Locomotives**: $\frac{330}{406} = \mathbf{81.28\%}$ availability ($76$ inoperable units; **$18.72\%$ out of service**).
- **Diesel Fleet (KRD Trains)**: $\frac{77}{85} = \mathbf{90.59\%}$ availability ($8$ inoperable units; **$9.41\%$ out of service**).
- **Freight Cars (_Gerbong Barang_)**: $\frac{3,376}{6,823} = \mathbf{49.48\%}$ availability ($3,447$ inoperable units; **$50.52\%$ grounded**—crippling bulk freight cash flow).
- **Local Train Service**: Collapsed from $74$ down to $17$ operational units $= \frac{17}{74} = \mathbf{22.97\%}$ availability ($57$ units lost; **$77.03\%$ capacity collapse**).
- **Total Fleet Inventory**: Across all $7,388$ units ($406 + 85 + 6,823 + 74$), only $3,800$ functioned (**$51.43\%$ availability**; **$48.57\%$ inoperable**), directly explaining consecutive losses of Rp 83.5B (2007–2008).

### 3. Defending the Root Cause (Strict 95 Words)

> We judge **P9 (absent leadership/vision)** and **P10 (weak corporate governance)** as the structural root of the remaining eight problems. Inheriting a colonial bureaucratic model, KAI operated without customer-centric orientation, believing the government—not passengers—fed them (p. 8). This leadership vacuum directly fostered **P6** (low pay, e.g., Senior Station Heads earning Rp 2.75M, breeding moonlighting and corruption) and **P5** (unresponsive HR and lack of discipline), while ignoring IT (**P8**), allowing scalpers to capture revenue. The resulting commercial hemorrhage (**P7**: Rp 83.5B losses) starved capex, triggering severe fleet and infrastructure deterioration (**P1–P3**) and making chronic delays (**P4**) systemic.

## 🎙️ Part 4: Analytical Synthesis & Classroom Cold-Call Defense

### 1. The Core Marketing Dilemma: The "Making Intangibles Tangible" Paradox

Why did Jonan start with the **toilets** instead of locomotive procurement or signaling software?

- **Professor Prompt:** _"Syndicate 9, KAI had Rp 83.5 billion in losses and 50% of its freight trains were broken down. Why did Jonan waste his first 100 days cleaning toilets?"_
- **Executive Cold-Call Response:** _"Because rail transport is an **experience and credence service** (Zeithaml, 1981; Slides 20–25). Passengers cannot open the engine hood to inspect GE locomotive reliability, nor can they verify track signaling. They evaluate unobservable technical competence through **tangible proxies** in the servicescape. If KAI cannot even keep a station toilet clean and smelling fresh, passengers instantly infer that KAI cannot keep its trains safe or punctual. Cleaning toilets was the lowest-cost, highest-visibility physical evidence that signaled the death of the old culture and restored passenger trust."_

### 2. The Services Marketing Triangle Synergy (Slide 31)

Service excellence cannot be delivered through advertising alone. External marketing (_promises made_) collapses without **Internal Marketing** (_enabling promises_) and **Interactive Marketing** (_keeping promises_):

- If KAI had launched online advertising (`External Marketing`) while Station Heads earned an unlivable Rp 2.75 million, employees would continue selling seats to brokers under the table, destroying `Interactive Marketing` at the boarding gate.
- Raising pay tenfold to Rp 30 million, establishing a Whistleblowing System, and instilling customer accountability (`Internal Marketing`) gave frontline workers the dignity and economic security to deliver flawless customer service.
