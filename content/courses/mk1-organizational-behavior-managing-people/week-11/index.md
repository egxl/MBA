# Week 11: Talent Management, Development & Succession

## 🎯 Executive Summary

Institutional continuity depends on the deliberate grooming of high-potential talent and robust succession planning. Organizations that fail to build deep leadership bench strength face acute enterprise fragility when key executives depart or business models rapidly evolve.

---

## 1. Core Frameworks & Theoretical Foundations

### The 9-Box Talent Matrix (Performance vs. Potential)

A diagnostic grid for portfolio-based talent management:

- **X-Axis (Performance)**: Sustained track record of hitting operational and strategic targets (Low, Medium, High).
- **Y-Axis (Potential)**: Learning agility, emotional quotient (EQ), capacity for handling broader mandates and ambiguity (Low, Medium, High).
- **Key Interventions**:
  - _Star Performers (High-High)_: Accelerated rotation, board-level exposure, executive coaching, tailored retention packages.
  - _Core Workhorses (High Performance, Medium/Low Potential)_: Deep subject matter mastery, operational recognition, stability anchors.
  - _Action Plan / Red Zone (Low-Low)_: Explicit performance turnaround milestones or managed off-ramps.

### The Leadership Pipeline Model (Charan, Drotter, Noel)

Managing critical career transitions:

1. Managing Self $\to$ Managing Others
2. Managing Others $\to$ Managing Managers
3. Managing Managers $\to$ Functional Manager
4. Functional Manager $\to$ Business Manager (P&L ownership)
5. Business Manager $\to$ Enterprise Executive

---

## 2. Executive Takeaways

- De-biasing talent reviews: Calibrating talent across departments through consensus calibration committees rather than unilateral supervisor rankings.
- High-stakes succession management: Preparing at least 2–3 viable successors (Ready Now, Ready in 1–2 Years, Emergency Backup) for all mission-critical C-suite roles.
