### Question 1: Extrinsic vs. Intrinsic Motivation

**Prompt**: A high-tech enterprise replaces flexible project allocation with a strict monthly sales quota paired with large cash bonuses. Within three quarters, voluntary turnover among elite software architects surges by 35%. Which psychological principle best accounts for this outcome?

- **A)** The Hawthorne Effect
- **B)** The Overjustification Effect (Crowding out of intrinsic autonomy) _(Correct)_
- **C)** Maslow's Physiological Deficiency
- **D)** Expectancy Theory Valency Collapse

> [!quiz] Behavioral Rationale
> Under Self-Determination Theory and the **Overjustification Effect**, when substantial external contingent rewards are introduced for tasks that individuals already find intrinsically rewarding, psychological perceptions of autonomy decline. The activity shifts from being perceived as self-directed to being externally controlled, diminishing creative engagement and discretionary effort among knowledge workers.

### Question 2: Managerial Biases

**Prompt**: An executive steering committee continues funding an enterprise ERP deployment despite being 18 months behind schedule and $14M over budget. Key stakeholders defend their position by arguing: _"We have already invested too much capital and reputation to abandon ship now."_ What bias dominates here?

- **A)** Anchoring and Adjustment Bias
- **B)** Sunk Cost Fallacy (Escalation of Commitment) _(Correct)_
- **C)** Availability Heuristic
- **D)** Framing Effect

> [!quiz] Behavioral Rationale
> The **Sunk Cost Fallacy** causes decision makers to incorporate unrecoverable historical costs into forward-looking decisions. Rational capital allocation evaluates only incremental prospective benefits versus incremental prospective costs.

### Question 3: Kerr's Folly in Retail Operations

**Prompt**: Referring to the Walmart case study, Sam Walton instituted store-level shrinkage bonuses rather than individual loss prevention metrics. Why did this design succeed?

- **A)** It eliminated the need for store managers to monitor inventory.
- **B)** It converted loss prevention into a collective team norm, aligning individual peer monitoring with enterprise EBITDA. _(Correct)_
- **C)** It violated Herzberg's hygiene principles to reduce costs.
- **D)** It incentivized associates to conceal theft from executive audits.

> **Detailed Rationale**:
> Individualized quotas often produce finger-pointing and siloing. Group-level incentives linked directly to store shrinkage created mutual accountability ("associates checking associates"), converting loss prevention into a shared cultural norm.
