## 👤 Executive / Enterprise Profile

**Richard B. Chase** is Professor Emeritus of Operations Management at the **Marshall School of Business, University of Southern California (USC)**. He is widely celebrated as one of the founding fathers of modern service operations management and service system architecture.

Along with co-author **F. Robert Jacobs** (Indiana University), Chase co-authored the seminal textbook _Operations and Supply Chain Management_ (McGraw-Hill), which has served as the definitive foundational curriculum for MBA programs globally for over four decades. He has served as a visiting professor at Harvard Business School, IMD Lausanne, and UCLA Anderson.

## 🧠 Core Philosophy & Contributions

### 1. The Customer Contact Approach to Service Design

In his groundbreaking 1978 Harvard Business Review article (_"Where Does the Customer Fit in a Service Operation?"_), Chase introduced the **Customer Contact Approach**, revolutionizing how managers analyze service facilities:

- Unlike manufacturing plants where the product is separate from the buyer, service systems inherently operate with the customer inside the delivery mechanism.
- The degree of customer contact dictates the system's operational efficiency: high-contact operations face continuous disruption and variability, whereas low-contact operations can be decoupled and engineered like high-efficiency factories.

### 2. The Service-System Design Matrix

Chase formalized the relationship between customer contact, operational efficiency, sales opportunity, and worker skill requirements into the **Service-System Design Matrix**:

- Categorized service encounters into **Buffered Core** (mail, automated kiosks), **Permeable Systems** (phone, structured face-to-face), and **Reactive Systems** (customized, high-contact encounters).
- Demonstrated that automated systems filter out routine work, leaving remaining frontline personnel with higher cognitive and diagnostic loads.

### 3. Service Poka-Yokes (Failsafing the Encounter)

Chase extended Shigeo Shingo's manufacturing _poka-yoke_ concepts into service delivery, formulating the **Three Ts of Service Failsafing**:

1. **The Task**: Ensuring the service activity is performed correctly (e.g., kitting parts, indented surgical trays).
2. **The Treatment**: Ensuring interpersonal customer interactions remain respectful and empathetic (e.g., eye contact mirrors, scripted greeting sequences).
3. **The Tangibles**: Ensuring physical evidence reinforces quality and clarity (e.g., turnstiles, take-a-number systems, height bars at theme parks).
