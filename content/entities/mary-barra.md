## 👤 Executive Profile

**Mary Teresa Barra** (born December 24, 1961) is an American business executive who has served as the Chair and Chief Executive Officer of General Motors (GM) since January 2014. She is the first female chief executive of a "Big Three" American automaker and one of the most prominent transformational leaders in global industrial history.

A lifelong GM insider who began her career with the company at age 18 as a co-op engineering student at the Pontiac Motor Division in 1980, Barra rose through plant management, internal communications, executive vice president of global product development, and head of human resources before being appointed CEO.

## 🧠 Core Leadership Breakthroughs & Culture Transformation

### 1. Dismantling the "GM Salute" and "GM Nod"

Immediately after taking office in 2014, Barra faced GM's catastrophic **ignition switch safety crisis**, linked to over 100 fatalities and millions of recalled vehicles. An independent investigation led by Anton Valukas revealed deep cultural dysfunction characterized by two institutional pathologies:

- **The "GM Salute"**: An executive gesture where managers crossed their arms and pointed outward, signaling that responsibility belonged to another department or silo.
- **The "GM Nod"**: Meetings where executives nodded in unanimous agreement with a directive, only to leave the room and take zero action.

Barra addressed Congress, openly apologized to victim families, fired 15 senior executives, and spearheaded a radical cultural turnaround encapsulated in the operating maxim:

> _"Say the thing you think you cannot say."_

She replaced GM's 10-page bureaucratic employee dress code policy with a two-word directive: _"Dress appropriately"_, signaling trust and adult accountability across the workforce.

### 2. The Electric & Autonomous Vehicle Pivot (Zero Crashes, Zero Emissions, Zero Congestion)

Barra re-anchored GM’s corporate mission around the visionary triad: **"Zero Crashes, Zero Emissions, Zero Congestion"**. Major strategic moves included:

- Committing **\$35 billion** through 2025 to electric vehicle and autonomous vehicle development.
- Architecting the modular **Ultium Battery Platform**, enabling flexible EV manufacturing across pickup trucks, luxury SUVs, and commercial vans.
- Acquiring and funding autonomous mobility startup **Cruise**.
- Pledging to eliminate tailpipe emissions from all new light-duty vehicles by **2035**, positioning GM as a direct challenger to Tesla and Chinese EV entrants.
