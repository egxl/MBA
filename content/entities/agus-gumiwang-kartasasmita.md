## 👤 Executive Profile

**Dr. Agus Gumiwang Kartasasmita, M.Si.** is an Indonesian statesman, political economist, and senior public-sector architect serving as **Menteri Perindustrian Republik Indonesia** (Minister of Industry). Entrusted with overseeing the industrial portfolio across successive cabinet administrations (Kabinet Indonesia Maju 2019–2024 and Kabinet Merah Putih 2024–2029), he serves as the primary executive driver of Indonesia's national industrialization agenda, commodity down-streaming (_hilirisasi_), domestic component enhancement (_P3DN / TKDN_), and manufacturing structural transformation toward **Indonesia Emas 2045**.

Prior to leading the Ministry of Industry, Agus Gumiwang built an extensive legislative and cabinet career. He served as **Menteri Sosial RI** (2018–2019) and represented West Java in the House of Representatives (**DPR RI**) across multiple terms (2009–2018), where he focused on defense, foreign affairs, intelligence, and state budgets. He earned his bachelor's degree in Commercial Science from Northeastern University in Boston, followed by master's and doctoral degrees in government and political science from Universitas Padjadjaran (UNPAD), Bandung. Combining deep institutional statecraft with macroeconomic rigor, he advocates for an industrial strategy deeply anchored in **Ekonomi Pancasila**—one that balances market productivity with social justice and constitutional sovereignty.

## 🧠 Core Philosophy & Policy Contributions

### 1. _Bhinneka Tunggal Ika_ & The 5-Pillar Industrial Ecosystem

Agus Gumiwang rejects the simplistic notion that industrialization merely equals constructing physical plants:

> _"Membangun sebuah pabrik adalah hal yang berbeda dengan menciptakan ekosistem Industri."_  
> — **Dr. Agus Gumiwang Kartasasmita, M.Si.**

Under his doctrine, authentic industrialization requires a symbiotic ecosystem anchored in the five radiating pillars of Ekonomi Pancasila:

1. **Gotong Royong & Social Justice**: Equitable distribution of manufacturing activity beyond Java.
2. **Kawasan Industri (KI) as Economic Anchors**: Integrated industrial zones providing plug-and-play utilities, logistical efficiency, and circular resource flows.
3. **Industrial Sovereignty & Worker Welfare**: Protecting domestic industrial workforce while upgrading labor skills.
4. **Domestic Value Addition (_Hilirisasi_)**: Banning raw commodity exports to capture intermediate and downstream manufacturing margins.
5. **Technical Human Capital (TVET/Vokasi) & Technology Transfer**: Closing the engineering competency gap through dual-system vocational education and R&D absorption.

### 2. P3DN & Multiplier TKDN (Anti-Leakage Doctrine)

To prevent national wealth and state budgets from leaking overseas through non-essential imports, Agus Gumiwang institutionalized the **Peningkatan Penggunaan Produk Dalam Negeri (P3DN)** regime. Supported by mandatory **Tingkat Komponen Dalam Negeri (TKDN)** certifications, state procurement across APBN, APBD, and BUMN holding companies must prioritize domestic goods. His empirical models demonstrate that every rupiah injected into certified domestic goods generates a substantial economic multiplier, expanding national GDP, creating formal industrial jobs, and securing fiscal revenue.

### 3. RIPIN 2025–2045 & The 8 Priority Industrial Sectors

In aligning national planning with President Prabowo Subianto's **Asta Cita** and the **Rencana Induk Pembangunan Industri Nasional (RIPIN 2025–2045)**, Agus Gumiwang set the structural target of lifting manufacturing's share of national GDP to **28%** by 2045. He spearheaded focused investment and technological deepening across eight priority sectors:

1. Pangan (Food security & agro-industry)
2. Farmasi, Kosmetik & Alat Kesehatan (Biotech & medical sovereignty)
3. Tekstil, Kulit & Alas Kaki (High-employment manufacturing & technical textiles)
4. Alat Transportasi (Electric vehicle ecosystem / KBLBB, rail, aviation, and maritime)
5. Elektronika & Telematika (Semiconductors, components, and digital hardware)
6. Pembangkit Energi (Renewable equipment, solar panels, and turbines)
7. Kimia Hulu-Hilir & Logam Dasar (Olefins, aromatics, steel, aluminum, copper)
8. Industri Bahan Galian Non-Logam (Advanced ceramics, cement, and construction materials)

### 4. Technical Barriers to Trade (TBT) Deficit Mitigation

Observing that peer economies like China deploy over 2,000 Technical Barriers to Trade (TBT) and the European Union enforces over 5,000 standards while Indonesia historically maintained only around 700 technical regulations, Agus Gumiwang instituted aggressive trade protection instruments. He introduced:

- **Restricted Ports of Entry**: Designating Eastern Indonesian ports (Bitung, Sorong, Kupang) as exclusive entry gates for seven sensitive import commodities (textiles, ceramics, footwear, electronics, apparel, cosmetics, traditional medicine) to curb illicit dumping in Java.
- **Mandatory SNI & Trade Remedies**: Accelerating Bea Masuk Tindakan Pengamanan (BMTP) and Bea Masuk Anti-Dumping (BMAD) to insulate domestic manufacturers against international predatory pricing.

### 5. BUMN as the Locomotive of Industrialization

Delivered during his foundation plenary to the **P3MD Batch I Soedirman** cohort, Agus Gumiwang positioned state-owned enterprises (BUMN) as the pivotal engine of national industrialization. Rather than competing destructively against private industry, BUMN must act as:

- **Primary Offtakers**: Providing guaranteed purchase contracts for domestic infant industries.
- **Anchor Tenants**: Developing critical utilities in integrated industrial estates.
- **Technology Vanguards**: Underwriting high-risk, capital-intensive basic research and industrial infrastructure (such as battery cells, defense electronics, and petrochemical crackers).
