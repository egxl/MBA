## 📐 Universal Metric & Mathematical Formula

A **Compa-Ratio** (Comparative Ratio) measures the relationship between an employee's actual compensation and the statistical midpoint of the target market salary band:

$$\text{Compa-Ratio} = \frac{\text{Actual Employee Base Salary}}{\text{Midpoint of Market Salary Band}}$$

### Standard Banding Thresholds:

- **$< 0.80$ (Under-Compensated)**: Severe flight risk for top performers; typically acceptable only during initial learning curves or new graduate onboarding.
- **$0.80 - 1.00$ (Market Competitive Entry)**: Standard progression band for developing executives.
- **$1.00$ (Market Parity)**: Exact alignment with market median for fully competent performers.
- **$1.00 - 1.20$ (Premium Performance)**: Retention pricing for critical talent density and high-impact specialists.
- **$> 1.20$ (Capped Range)**: Compensation red-flag; requires promotion to a higher band or conversion into performance-contingent equity/bonuses rather than increasing base fixed costs.

## 🏛️ Multi-Disciplinary Lenses

### 1. Human Capital & Retention Lens (MK 1 & Rabuan)

- **Primary Source**: [[rabuan/2026-09-02-adh-peoplemath|Agus Dwi Handaya's Rabuan Masterclass on PeopleMath]].
- **Retention Calculus**: When key performers fall below $0.85$ compa-ratio, voluntary turnover escalates exponentially. Replacing them costs $1.5\times-2.5\times$ annual salary in direct search, ramp-up drag, and team disruption.
- **Equity Governance**: Prevents wage compression where new outside hires enter at higher salaries than high-performing incumbents.

### 2. Financial Strategy Lens (MK 2)

- **Fixed Cost vs. Variable Leverage**: Managing fixed human capital overhead. Excessive base compa-ratios increase enterprise operating leverage and raise break-even operational thresholds during macroeconomic downturns.
